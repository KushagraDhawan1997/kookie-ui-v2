KookieUI optimizes for correctness, not adoption. Right, not popular — chosen deliberately,
because the market cannot perceive the axis being optimized, so the two diverge constantly.

The judges are the two things that cannot flatter: the human-factors literature, and whether
real products built on it are demonstrably better. Not stars. Not conviction alone.

## Patterns, not components

A **pattern** is a recurring functional structure — context, problem, solution. It exists in
the world; you discover it. A **component** is a pattern encapsulated for reuse. A **name** is
a handle.

The order matters, and it is the order almost nobody works in: pattern first, encapsulation
second, name last and least. The industry works in reverse — names and appearances first,
function last — and that inversion is the root of every failure below it.

## Function over appearance

A taxonomy has to carve reality at functional joints, not at appearances. The canonical case
in this system: filtering one view by different data pulls, and switching between peer views,
are distinct operations. They render as near-identical horizontal segments, and they are not
the same thing.

Collapsing them into one component with two variants is not an ugly choice. It is a false one.
Co-categorization manufactures perceived sameness — people compress within-group difference
and exaggerate between-group difference — so structure teaches whether you intend it or not,
and reducing function to appearance de-educates everyone downstream.

The library author is the one party obligated to know. That obligation is this whole document:
encode the real distinctions, teach them, and trust the taught.

## Design is empirical

Much of design has objective floors. Fitts's law is from 1954 and it predicts movement time
from distance and target width. Touch anthropometry puts a fingerpad near 10mm, which is where
every platform's 44-point minimum comes from. The Gestalt grouping principles are the
documented behaviour of the visual grouping system, published in 1923. Contrast has two
measurable models and this system uses both.

Above those floors is taste within constraints — the exact radius, the material's saturation,
the specific accent. The discipline is knowing which is which: cite the floors and be
immovable about them, own the taste as judgment, and never dress one as the other. Asserting
false precision is the same sin as asserting false sameness, inverted.

## Coherence is negative-cost

Coherence is *cheaper* than incoherence. Shipping the same button twice is easier than
re-deciding its weight. Incoherence is therefore not saved time — it is re-derivation without
memory.

A centerless system re-decides every choice locally, in ignorance of the choices already made,
and local optima do not compose. The cost is real and it is deferred: it is paid at assembly
scale, by whoever builds the twentieth screen.

## Why the field went this way

Not incompetence. Incentives.

- **Centerless tools won.** Copy-paste component collections have no center by construction.
  Utility CSS pushes decisions out to the call site by design. Both traded coherence for
  adoption velocity, on purpose, and both were right about what the market rewards.
- **Component-thinking got applied to patterns.** A sidebar is a pattern — its essence is
  outward coordination — not a large component, whose essence is inward containment. The tell
  of the category error is a child reaching up to configure its parent.
- **The talent is hidden and mis-selected.** Systematic designers work on private systems
  inside companies. The public market selects for virality, which rewards isolable, demoable
  craft and is blind to coherence, because coherence is only visible at assembly.
- **The human-factors literature was abandoned**, because consumer web has no forcing
  function. A missed tap is cheap, so "responsiveness" degraded from *adapt to the human on
  this device* into *reflow without a scrollbar* — the measurable proxy replacing the goal.
- **AI industrialized incoherence.** Each component is generated in isolation, locally
  plausible and globally inconsistent, and it looks fine per component, so no alarm fires.

## What this costs you

An honest cap, stated once. A system that optimizes for correctness is harder to adopt than
one that optimizes for adoption. There is no `variant` prop to reach for when the design does
not fit; there are refusals with reasons instead. Fewer escape hatches exist, and the ones
that do are documented rather than incidental.

The trade is deliberate. What you get for it is a system where the twentieth screen costs what
the second one did.
