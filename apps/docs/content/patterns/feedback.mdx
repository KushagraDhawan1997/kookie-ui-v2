An interface has to say four things about work it is doing: that it started, how far it has got,
how it ended, and what went wrong. Each has one answer here, and the usual failure is not an
ugly one — it is giving the wrong answer, a spinner where the fraction is known, or a colour
where a sentence was needed.

## The four things, and what answers each

| what you are saying | when | the answer |
|---|---|---|
| Busy | Work started and you cannot say how long | `Spinner`, or `loading` on the `Button` that started it |
| Progress | You know the fraction | `Progress` with a `value` |
| Outcome | It finished | Words where the action was, in the tone that names the result |
| Error | It failed, or the input was wrong | The control's own invalid state, plus a sentence |

Outcome is the one with no component yet, and that is stated plainly at the end of this chapter
rather than papered over.

## Busy never costs the label

`loading` on a `Button` swaps the leading slot for a `Spinner`. The glyph's box is reused, so
nothing shifts, and the label stays — a button that stops saying what it is doing is worse than
one that changes width.

```tsx
<Button emphasis="loud" loading={saving}>Save changes</Button>
```

While it is loading the control keeps its focus and its place in the tab order, announces
`aria-busy`, and refuses activation. The `Spinner` inside it is hidden from assistive technology
on purpose: the control already carries the state, and saying it twice is noise.

Standalone, a `Spinner` is for a region that has nothing else to show yet. It fills with
`currentColor` and takes the icon box of whatever it sits in, so it needs neither a colour nor a
size of its own.

## Progress when the fraction is known

`Progress` is a well and a fill. A number reports the fraction; `value={null}` sweeps as an
indeterminate bar. The prop is required by the type, so "I do not know how far along this is"
has to be said out loud rather than being what you get by forgetting.

It has no `size`, and the absence is a decision rather than a gap: the ladder it might have
ridden prices a track as a fraction of a slider's thumb, and a bar has no thumb, so there is
nothing for a size index to be a fraction of. Its extent comes from the container, so the `Box`
that owns the row decides how wide it is.

```tsx
<Stack gap="2">
  <Progress value={uploaded} aria-label="Uploading files" />
  <Text size="2" emphasis="medium">14 of 24 files</Text>
</Stack>
```

Name it. A bar with no accessible name and no heading wired to it is a number nobody can read.
Prefer the determinate form wherever the fraction exists at all — an indeterminate sweep says
"something is happening", which is the least a busy indicator can say.

## Motion that is the content slows, it does not stop

Everything else in this system stands down to stillness under `prefers-reduced-motion`. A
spinner and an indeterminate bar are the exception, and the reason is what they are: their
movement is not a response to a state change, it *is* the message. Stopping them would remove
the only thing they were saying, so they slow instead.

That distinction is worth carrying into anything you compose. A skeleton shimmer, a pulsing dot
and a sweeping bar are all in this category; a hover fill, a panel opening and a ring landing are
not.

## Status tone is vocabulary, not palette

Four names carry meaning: `success`, `warning`, `info`, `destructive`. Four more are colour as
data — `blue`, `green`, `orange`, `amber` — for a chart series, a project label, a category,
where the colour *is* the information and a status word would be dishonest.

`success` and `green` render identically today and are still two separately generated families,
because a meaning has to stay independently correctable. Do not write `green` when you mean
success: it looks right, and it is wrong the first time either name is re-judged.

```tsx
// The thing people reach for
<Text size="2" tone="green">Deployed</Text>

// The composed answer
<Text size="2" tone="success">Deployed</Text>
```

Keep the count down as well. Four or more data tones on one surface is a palette being enjoyed
rather than a meaning being said; three is a legend — a status set, a three-series key.

## Colour is never the whole message

Colour alone is not sufficient signalling (WCAG 1.4.1), and every status this system paints is
half a message by design. The border, the ring and the ink are the system's half. The sentence
is yours.

A field that turns red with nothing beside it, a green dot with no label, a chart keyed only by
hue — all three fail the same way, and all three fail hardest for exactly the people the tone was
loudest for. Errors should also say what to do next, which is the part a colour could never have
carried even if it were enough.

## What has not shipped, and what to compose instead

`Callout`, `Banner` and `Toast` are specified as tone-forward surfaces and none of them exists
yet. Naming that here is better than a chapter that quietly implies otherwise, and there is a
reasonable answer for each case in the meantime.

- **An inline outcome.** Put the result where the action was: a `Text` in the matching tone
  beside or under the control, and move the verb's tense with it — a button that said "Publish"
  is followed by the word "Published", not by "Success".
- **A message with a heading.** Compose it — a `Card` holding a `Heading` and a `Text`, with the
  tone stamped on the words. Do not tint the card. The surface family takes no tone, and
  reaching for `style` to fake one bakes a pigment into an app that will then never re-theme.
- **A transient confirmation.** There is no toast, and a fixed-position `Card` is not one: it
  gets neither the live region that makes an announcement nor the dismissal behaviour people
  expect from it. Say the outcome inline instead. If it genuinely needs a decision rather than an
  acknowledgement, that was an `AlertDialog` all along.
