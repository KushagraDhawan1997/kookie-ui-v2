The material setting controls whether the panels in your app are opaque or made of glass. You
set it once on the `Theme`, and it applies only where content actually passes behind a
component, such as a toolbar over a canvas or a menu over the page.

## Set one material for the whole app

```tsx
<Theme material="regular">
  <App />
</Theme>
```

`material` accepts four values: `solid`, `thin`, `regular` and `thick`. The default is `solid`,
which lets no light through. The other three are glass. Each step lets less of the content
behind the glass through, so a thicker glass is more opaque. A menu or a popover also blurs the
content behind it a little more at each step.

The value does not change how the glass bends the content at its edge. The bend comes from the
shape of the component: a button is a small dome, a menu has a wider edge, and a card has a
gentler edge.

One value covers the whole app, but each kind of component uses it differently. A menu is
more opaque than a card at every value, because it holds rows of text over the content it
covers. A `Dialog` also feels heavier than a card, because it dims everything behind it.

No component accepts a `material` prop. To change the material for one part of your app, nest
a `<Theme material>` around that part, which is how every other Theme setting works as well.

## Mark where content passes behind

The Theme sets which material to use, while `backdrop` tells the system where to use it. Glass
over a plain background has nothing to show, but the browser still reads the background on
every repaint, so a control in the ordinary page flow stays solid unless you mark it.

Mark the region rather than each control. A `Box` with `backdrop` turns every control inside it
to glass:

```tsx
<Theme material="regular">
  <Box className="editor-canvas">
    <Box backdrop p="2">
      <Flex gap="2" align="center">
        <Button emphasis="quiet">Crop</Button>
        <Button emphasis="quiet">Rotate</Button>
        <Separator orientation="vertical" />
        <TextField size="2" placeholder="Layer name" />
        <Button emphasis="loud">Export</Button>
      </Flex>
    </Box>
  </Box>
</Theme>
```

Each control in this toolbar keeps its own emphasis and shows the artwork underneath. Set
`backdrop={false}` on a `Box` inside the region to make a smaller area plain again. `backdrop`
only says where a component sits. It never changes how thick the glass is.

You can also set `backdrop` on a single component. The prop is available on:

- **Surfaces:** `Card`, `Notice`, `Composer`, `Toolbar` and `ToolbarGroup`
- **Controls:** `Button`, `Toggle`, `SegmentedControl`, `SelectTrigger` and `MessageScrollerButton`
- **Fields:** `TextField`, `TextArea`, `NumberField` and `ComboboxInput`
- **Small items:** `Chip`, `Avatar` and `Attachment`
- **App frame panels:** `ShellHeader`, `ShellRail`, `ShellSidebar`, `ShellInspector` and
  `ShellBottom`

A sidebar that floats over a canvas is the clearest case for glass in an app. `ShellContent`
does not accept `backdrop`, because the work area is the bottom layer of the frame and nothing
passes behind it. To add a glass region inside the work area, place a `<Box backdrop>` or a
`<Card backdrop>` there.

## Panels that cover the page use glass automatically

A `Menu`, a `Select` list, a `Popover`, a `Dialog` and every other panel that opens over the
page always has content behind it, so it uses the theme's material without a prop. A `Shell`
panel that opens as a drawer on a narrow window does the same.

For every other component, the system picks the material in this order:

1. If the component sits inside a glass panel, it uses the panel's transparency with no glass
   effect of its own.
2. If you set `backdrop` on the component, that value decides.
3. If the component sits in a region marked with `backdrop`, it uses glass.
4. Otherwise, it stays `solid`.

## Glass doesn't stack

A component inside a glass panel does not become a second layer of glass. It paints its fill at
the panel's transparency and adds no glass effect of its own, and the system applies this for
you.

The reason is physical. A panel looks like glass because it filters the content behind it.
Inside another glass panel, that content has already been filtered once, so a second filter adds
cost and makes the inner panel look like a sticker.

A solid panel stops a marked region at its edge, so an unmarked control on an opaque card stays
solid. You can still put glass inside a solid card when something inside it moves behind a
control, such as a map or a feed. Mark that place explicitly:

```tsx
<Card>
  <Box backdrop>
    {/* your own map canvas paints here */}
    <Button>Recenter</Button>
  </Box>
</Card>
```

A nested `<Theme>` also resets the region. A menu opened from a glass card is glass again,
because it paints over the page rather than inside the card.

To give your own element the same material, read it with `useMaterial` and put it on an element
with the surface class:

```tsx
const material = useMaterial({ backdrop: true });
return <div className="kui-surface" data-material={material} />;
```

## Glass keeps the component's colour

Glass changes how a fill is painted rather than replacing it. The system takes the colour the
component would paint and makes it translucent, so your `tone` and `emphasis` still apply. A loud button on
glass is a translucent version of its solid fill, a medium button is a soft tint, and a quiet button has no fill at all. Each one keeps its own label colour.

In Chromium browsers, the edge of a glass panel also bends the content behind it, like the edge
of a thick lens. Other browsers show the translucent fill and the edge highlight without the bend.

## When to use glass

Use glass where content passes behind a panel:

- a toolbar over a canvas
- a panel over a hero image
- a header that content scrolls under

Glass costs a background read for every panel on every repaint. Fixed chrome is the best case,
because a glass panel that moves must read its background again on every frame. Don't mark a
region that sits over a plain background, because the glass has nothing to show there and still
costs the read. Set `material="solid"` on a nested Theme to remove the cost from one region.

Two accessibility settings change the glass for you:

- **Reduced transparency:** Every glass panel becomes solid.
- **Higher contrast:** Every thickness becomes much more opaque, while thin, regular and thick
  stay in the same order. The edge highlight and the bent edge stay the same, because they paint
  inside the panel and cannot lower the contrast of its text.
