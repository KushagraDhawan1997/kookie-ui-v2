You name what a colour means, and the theme calculates the value. The value changes with the
light or dark mode, the contrast setting, and the surface under it.

The theme generates every value from two numbers for each colour family. You write no hex
values. You do not have to remember a palette.

## Say what you mean, not which colour

```tsx
// Do not do this.
<Text style={{ color: "#E5484D" }}>Payment failed</Text>

// Do this.
<Text tone="destructive">Payment failed</Text>
```

A hex value never changes. Thus it has four problems:

- It doesn't change in dark mode.
- It doesn't change with the high-contrast setting.
- It doesn't change when you change your brand colour.
- It doesn't blend with the panel behind glass.

`tone="destructive"` solves all four problems, because the theme produces the value.

There's no `color` or `variant` prop. If you must set a colour yourself, use `style`. A `style`
value is easy to see in a review. If you often write `style` for colour, a colour family is
missing. Add that family to the configuration.

## The ten families

All families use the same lightness scale. Thus step 9 of red and step 9 of blue have the same
lightness.

| tone | what it is for |
|---|---|
| `neutral` | Text, hairlines, wells, and all items that have no meaning of their own. This is most of an interface. |
| `accent` | Your brand. Use it at full strength one time on each screen. |
| `blue` `green` `orange` `amber` | Colour as data: a chart series, a project label, or a category. The colour *is* the information. |
| `destructive` | The action that removes something, and the error state. |
| `success` `warning` `info` | Status: the action worked, the item needs attention, or the text gives some context. |

If the item has a meaning, such as an error, use a meaning name. If the item has only a
category, such as one series in a chart, use a category name.

### Success and green are separate families

Today `success` and `green` show the same value. But they are two separate families. Thus you
can change each meaning independently. You can move `success` toward teal, and the green in
your chart does not change. `accent` and `blue` work the same way. If you change your brand to
green, you change `accent` and `blue` stays the same.

Do not write `tone="green"` when you mean success. The screen looks correct today. It becomes
incorrect the first time the value of either family changes.

## Components use roles, not steps

The twelve-step scale is public, and you can read a step from it. Components never read a step.
They read named roles. Thus when you change a tone, all the values change together.

```css
--accent-soft / -soft-hover / -soft-active      /* tints */
--accent-solid / -solid-hover / -solid-active   /* fills */
--accent-border
--accent-text                                   /* links and prose on a tint */
--accent-contrast                               /* the readable text on a solid fill */
--accent-ink / -ink-muted / -ink-faint          /* the three text colours */
--accent-glyph                                  /* icons and small marks */
--accent-a1 … --accent-a12                      /* semi-transparent, blends over the surface */
```

Each family has the same set of roles. `--destructive-solid`, `--success-ink`, and
`--warning-border` all exist. One role name works for all ten families.

## Tints and borders are grey in every family

A tinted fill is almost transparent. At 11% opacity, you see mostly the page behind the fill.
Thus a coloured tint gives very little information. For this reason, tinted fills are **grey in
every family**. A medium button, a quiet button under the pointer, and a chip are grey for all
tones. The family shows in the *text*.

Borders use the same rule, because a pale coloured border also gives very little information.
On a button, `bordered` is a step of loudness. The order is quiet, then quiet with an outline,
then medium. This order works the same in grey.

Checkbox borders and field borders do not change. They use a grey line of their own, and that
line never came from a family.

Solid fills keep their colour.

## The accent goes grey in two more places

Two items keep their colour for the other nine families, but are grey for the accent:

- the tinted background on a panel
- the two quieter text colours

Thus a `destructive` Notice is still a box with a red tint. A `destructive` paragraph at medium
is still a quieter red. An `accent` Notice and an `accent` paragraph are grey.

The other nine families have meanings, and a pale red still looks like danger. The accent is
your brand colour. An 11% tint of your brand colour does not look like your brand. For this
reason, the accent is grey where the meanings keep their colour.

## Icons use their own colour

Icons use `--{tone}-glyph`. This colour has the hue of the family at full saturation. Its
lightness makes a small mark easy to see on the background of each mode.

Two simpler solutions do not work:

- A **fill** cannot be an icon colour. A fill has one value in both modes, and no single colour
  is easy to see on both white and black.
- **Text colour** is easy to see on all backgrounds. But the theme calculates it for reading,
  and that higher standard decreases saturation. An accent icon in the text colour becomes a
  muted navy, and not the colour that you selected.

The glyph colour is 1.3 to 2.2 times more saturated than the text colour. This is true in every
family and in both modes.

A grey has no saturation to keep. Thus the glyph colour of a grey is its text colour.

## The three text colours

On text, `emphasis` selects one of three colours:

- Loud is `--color-text`.
- Medium is `--color-text-muted`.
- Quiet is `--color-text-faint`.

If you add a `tone`, the same three levels move to that family.

```tsx
<Card size="3">
  <Stack gap="3">
    <Flex gap="2" align="center" justify="space-between">
      <Heading size="6">Invoice 4021</Heading>
      <Text size="2" tone="destructive" weight="medium">Payment failed</Text>
    </Flex>
    <Text size="3">Your card was declined on 3 August.</Text>
    <Text size="3" emphasis="medium">We retry once more on 10 August.</Text>
    <Text size="2" emphasis="quiet">Refunds are not available on this plan.</Text>
    <Flex gap="3">
      <Button emphasis="loud" tone="destructive">Update card</Button>
      <Button emphasis="quiet" bordered>Contact support</Button>
    </Flex>
  </Stack>
</Card>
```

The heading and the first line are loud. The retry date is real information, but the screen
does not emphasise it. Thus it is medium. The refund line is a rule that nobody can act on. Thus
it is quiet. One tone goes to both the small label and the button, because every family has the
same roles.

The theme calculates each level against a contrast target. It does this one time for each family
and one time for each mode. Thus every family has the same contrast at the same level.

- **The theme does not calculate loud against a target.** Loud stays the designed text colour of
  the family. Loud is the colour that the system uses most, so a target number does not control
  it.
- **Medium is exactly on the minimum contrast for reading.** This minimum is APCA Lc 60, a
  measured limit. Use medium for real information that the screen does not emphasise: a
  placeholder, a timestamp, or a group label.
- **Quiet is below that minimum intentionally.** Quiet is Lc 30. Use it for an item that the
  system has disabled, such as an option that nobody can select.

Do not set a full paragraph in quiet. If text is less important, keep it loud at a smaller size,
or make it medium.

## The high-contrast setting

`contrast="high"` is a Theme setting. It is not a prop on each component. It also follows the
operating system. Thus if a person asks their system for more contrast, they get it, and you do
nothing.

```tsx
<Theme contrast="high">{children}</Theme>
```

A border or fill at rest is decoration. Decoration is a judgment of taste, and usually no
minimum contrast applies to it. Under `contrast="high"`, decoration must meet measured minimums.
Text, the focus ring, and the error border meet their minimums at every setting, in both modes.
These three items carry information.

The setting gives each colour as much contrast as that colour can hold. It does not add a fixed
amount to all colours. The border of a bright hue is already at its limit. More contrast turns
yellow into olive. Thus a bright hue gets its added contrast in the text. Some parts of some
colours do not change under the setting, and this is intentional.

## The three background colours

Three named backgrounds are under all other items. From the bottom up, they are:

```css
--color-page      /* your app's background. No component reads it. */
--color-ground    /* a bounded region that holds objects */
--color-surface   /* an object's own opaque fill */
```

<ColorGrounds />

The library paints no page background. If a Theme painted one, it would paint the page again
each time you put a Theme inside another Theme. Paint the page background yourself, and use
`--color-page` for the value.

`--color-ground` is the region under an object, such as a sidebar background or an area that
holds cards. `--color-surface` is the object itself, and it is opaque. Only the material setting
makes a surface transparent.

Light mode and dark mode put these three colours in a different order. This changes how you
compose a screen.

**In light mode, the page and a card are the same white.** A card directly on the page has no
fill that makes it different. Its shadow separates it from the page. In a flat theme, its
hairline separates it. Apple's platforms work the same way in light mode. For this reason,
`depth` is more important in light mode than in dark mode. A ground is one step darker than the
page and the card. Thus only the ground is different by colour.

**In dark mode, all three colours are different.** The page is the darkest, the ground is
lighter, and a card is the lightest. Each step is small and intentional. The ground has its own
selected value, and not a step of the palette. The nearest palette step was lighter
than the card. With that step, cards would look darker than the region that holds them.
