Motion here describes real travel and nothing else. It runs on two clocks that are different
kinds of thing — one for what a control says, one for how it moves — and no component owns a
duration. All of it is CSS: nothing runs JavaScript while your finger is down.

## Two clocks

**Paint is a signal. Geometry is physics.** Colour and opacity ease, and they are short:
`--motion-duration` and `--motion-easing`. Geometry rides a damped spring baked into a CSS
`linear()` curve — the config states the damping ratio and the frequency, the generator samples
the model, and the emitted curve costs the browser exactly what a cubic-bezier costs.

Running both on one clock is what makes an interface read as a slideshow. Keep them apart and a
control answers you immediately, then arrives.

```css
/* Reached for: one clock for everything. */
.thing {
  transition: all 200ms ease;
}

/* Here: the signal eases, the travel springs. */
.thing {
  transition:
    background-color var(--motion-duration) var(--motion-easing),
    translate var(--motion-press) var(--motion-spring-stiff);
}
```

## The press keeps its colour

A tap lasts about 60 milliseconds. An eased fill never reaches its colour inside one, so an eased
press reads as a dead control on a phone — measured on real hardware, invisible under desktop
emulation, and it cost this system six days of no motion at all before the split resolved it.
**A press sets its paint clock to zero.** The fill lands on the first frame while the travel
springs underneath it. That is a floor, not a preference: whatever motion arrives later, press
stays instant.

Hover is asymmetric — 80 milliseconds in, 220 out. That one is taste, and the reason is honest
asymmetry: arriving is something you did, leaving is something you stopped doing.

## Each family says how far it moves

Under a finger a Button sinks, down and smaller — down alone reads as sliding, smaller alone as
receding. An interactive surface sinks too, at its own distances: scale is relative, so a large
card at the button's factor would move several pixels per edge and read as the page flexing. A
mark squashes, because a Checkbox has no depth to sink into. A field does nothing at all — it is
the one control the eye rests inside, and its box never travels or scales.

**Nobody says how long.** The clock belongs to the shared layer and the distance to the family,
which is what keeps a press one sentence per component and keeps two controls at the same rung
moving alike.

```tsx
// This is the whole motion API. Both of these already move correctly.
<Button emphasis="loud">Deploy</Button>
<Card render={<button type="button" />}>Choose this plan</Card>
```

There is no duration prop, no easing prop and nothing named `animate`. A component cannot ease
something on its own authority either: a duration cannot be written down, because every channel
of every transition has to resolve to a motion token, so a hand-typed `150ms` fails in CI rather
than in an audit six weeks later.

## Damping is sacred

Both shipped curves cross their target at most once. A second crossing is a ring, and a ringing
interface reads mechanical — that failure killed three earlier spellings of the switch.

**When a movement needs to be more visible, give it more travel, never less damping.** This was
learned by falsification: short-travel controls looked dead, their curves were loosened, and the
result read as *just spring*. The bouncier curve exists in the lab as the rejected comparison and
is deliberately not emitted.

Some amplitudes are floors rather than taste. The focus ring's travel is bounded below by the
engine at about four pixels, because Chrome resolves `outline-offset` to whole pixels: a ring
with two pixels of room animates in three frames and reads as a stutter. A field's ring is
therefore instant, and that is a measurement, not a preference.

## Motion that is the content

A Spinner and an indeterminate Progress are a different category. Their movement is not a
transition between two states — it *is* the information, which is why they answer reduced motion
differently from everything else.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` they **slow**, the Spinner to three seconds, and never
stop. A busy indicator that stops moving is information lost.

## Reduced motion is total

Everything else stops. A panel is simply there and then simply gone, no control travels, and the
measurement that exists only to serve an animation is not taken. **A suppression that leaves one
channel moving is worse than none:** somebody who asked their operating system for stillness gets
it in pieces, which is the one outcome that setting cannot tolerate.

Structure carries it rather than arithmetic. Which arrival a control gets is a hook, and reduced
motion stands the *hook* down instead of the rules that read it, so a recipe and its suppression
share a selector and specificity can never separate them again. It is spelled that way because it
once was not: `:not()` takes the specificity of its most specific argument rather than summing the
list, and for six days the focus ring went on landing for exactly the people who had asked it not
to. The sheet that declares a clock now stands it down itself, on the declaring selector.

## Nothing runs while you are pressing

Hover, press, focus and the validity states are stylesheet work reading data attributes off the
element. There is no handler measuring anything as you point at a control, which is why a system
this animated still ships no runtime motion library.

The exceptions exist and none of them is at interaction time: a floating panel measures its
destination once per open, which is already a scripted moment; a tab bar re-measures its indicator
when the selection changes; the glass lens is built on mount and on resize. Pointing, pressing and
focusing reach no JavaScript at all.
