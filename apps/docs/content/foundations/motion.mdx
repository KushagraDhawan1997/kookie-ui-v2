Motion in this system covers two things: a colour change and a movement. Two separate clocks
drive them. No component sets a duration. CSS resolves every transition, so no JavaScript runs
while the user presses a control.

## Two clocks

A colour change or an opacity change eases, and it is short. It reads `--motion-duration` and
`--motion-easing`.

A movement follows a damped spring. The config states the damping ratio and the frequency. The
generator samples that physical model and bakes the samples into a CSS `linear()` curve. The
emitted curve costs the browser what a `cubic-bezier` curve costs.

Keep the two clocks apart. One clock for both channels makes the colour wait for the movement,
and the control then answers late.

```css
/* Wrong: one clock for every channel. */
.thing {
  transition: all 200ms ease;
}

/* Correct: the colour eases, the movement springs. */
.thing {
  transition:
    background-color var(--motion-duration) var(--motion-easing),
    translate var(--motion-press) var(--motion-spring-stiff);
}
```

## A press lands its colour immediately

A tap lasts about 60 milliseconds. An eased fill does not reach its colour inside that time. A
person on a phone then gets no colour answer from the tap. This is a measured limit, taken on
real hardware. Desktop emulation does not show the fault.

A press sets its paint clock to zero. The fill lands on the first frame. The movement springs
underneath it. This rule is a floor. Any motion added later keeps the press immediate.

Hover is asymmetric. It arrives in 80 milliseconds and leaves in 220 milliseconds. This is a
judgment. Arriving is an act of the user, and leaving is the end of one.

## Each family sets its own distance

A Button sinks under a press. It moves down, and it also becomes smaller. A downward move alone
looks like a slide. A size change alone looks like the Button retreating. The Button does both.

An interactive surface sinks with its own distances. Scale is relative, so a large Card at the
Button's factor would move each edge by several pixels. The user reads that as the page bending.

A mark squashes instead. A Checkbox has no depth to sink into. A field does not move at all. Its
box never travels and never scales, because the eye rests inside a field.

No family sets a duration. The shared layer owns the clock, and the family owns the distance.
Two controls at the same size therefore move alike.

This is the whole motion API. Every control below already moves correctly, and no line sets a
duration, a distance or an easing.

```tsx
<Card size="3">
  <Stack gap="4">
    <Heading size="6">Deploy to production</Heading>

    <Flex render={<label />} align="center" gap="3">
      <Switch size="2" />
      <Text size="3">Run the migration first</Text>
    </Flex>

    <Slider defaultValue={[25]} aria-label="Rollout percentage" />

    <Flex gap="3" justify="flex-end">
      <Button emphasis="quiet" bordered>Cancel</Button>
      <Button emphasis="loud">Deploy</Button>
    </Flex>
  </Stack>
</Card>
```

The buttons sink. The switch grip springs across its channel. The slider grip follows the
pointer. Each family sets its own distance, and the shared layer sets the clock.

There is no duration prop, no easing prop and no prop named `animate`. A component cannot write
a duration either. Every channel of every transition must resolve to a motion token, so a
hand-typed `150ms` fails CI.

## A movement crosses its target one time

Both curves cross their target one time at most. A second crossing is a wobble, and a wobbling
control looks mechanical.

To make a movement more visible, increase the distance. Do not reduce the damping. A looser
curve on a short travel reads as a spring effect and nothing else. Damping is fixed. Distance is
the variable a family owns.

## A focus ring cannot animate over a short distance

The focus ring cannot travel less than about four pixels. Chrome resolves `outline-offset` to
whole pixels, so a ring with two pixels of room animates in three frames. Three frames look like
a stutter. A field's ring is therefore immediate. This is a measured limit, not a judgment.

## Some motion carries the information

A Spinner and an indeterminate Progress belong to a different category. Their movement is not a
transition between two states. The movement is the information.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` both slow down, and the Spinner slows to three seconds.
Neither one stops. A busy indicator that stops loses the information it carries.

## Reduced motion stops everything else

Every other movement stops under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. A panel is present, and later
it is absent. No control travels. The system does not take a measurement that only serves an
animation.

A suppression that leaves one channel moving is worse than no suppression. A person who asks the
operating system for stillness must get all of it.

The stylesheet that starts a movement is also the one that stops it, on the same selector. A
new animation therefore cannot arrive without its own switch-off, and no CSS specificity fight
can leave one channel running.

## No JavaScript runs at interaction time

Hover, press, focus and the validity states are stylesheet rules that read data attributes off
the element. No handler measures anything while the user points at a control. The system ships
no runtime motion library.

Three exceptions exist, and none of them runs at interaction time. A floating panel measures its
destination one time per open. A tab bar measures its indicator again when the selection
changes. The glass lens is built on mount and on resize.
