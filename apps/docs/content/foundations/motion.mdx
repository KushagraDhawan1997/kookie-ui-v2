In Kookie, every change of state shows at once. There are no transitions, and panels have no
animation when they open or close. The only things that move are three busy indicators, because
their movement is the information they carry.

## Every change of state shows at once

When you hover, press or focus a control, the new state shows on the next frame. Nothing fades
between the old state and the new one, and the focus ring appears in its final position.

The states you set behave the same way. A checkbox shows its tick at once, a switch thumb
appears at the other end of its track, and the line under a tab appears under the selected tab.

This is a judgment. A change that shows at once is never late, so you see the result of a press
immediately, and nothing on the screen is caught halfway between two states.

## Controls stay still under the pointer

A control doesn't move or change size when you hover over it or press it. Only its colours
change. This applies to every control, including a `Card` that you render as a button or a
link, and a disabled control also keeps its place.

## Panels open and close at once

A `Menu`, a `Select` list, a `Popover` and a `Tooltip` appear in their final position. A
`Dialog`, an `AlertDialog` and a `Sheet` appear together with the dimmed background behind them.
Closing any of these panels removes it at once.

On a narrow window, a `Shell` panel opens as a drawer that pushes the content aside, and it also
appears and closes at once.

A tooltip waits a moment before it opens, so it stays hidden while your pointer passes over a
control on its way somewhere else. This wait is a delay rather than an animation.

## Elements you drag follow your pointer

The handle of a `Slider` stays under your pointer while you drag it, and a `Sheet` follows your
finger while you swipe it closed. This isn't animation, because the element moves only while
you move it.

Scrolling with a wheel, a trackpad or a finger is handled by the browser, and Kookie doesn't
change it. The buttons of a `Carousel` move its content at once.

## Three indicators move to show that work continues

These components move without any change of state, because the movement is their message:

- `Spinner`, which also shows inside a `Button` that has `loading`
- `Progress` with `value={null}`
- `Attachment`, while it works on a file and has no progress value

Use one of these when you cannot say how much of the work is done. Once you know the fraction,
give `Progress` a number. The bar then shows that fraction and changes only when the number
changes.

```tsx
<Card size="3">
  <Stack gap="4">
    <Heading size="6">Import files</Heading>
    <Progress value={null} aria-label="Importing files" />
    <Text size="2" emphasis="medium">Reading 24 files</Text>
    <Flex gap="3" justify="flex-end">
      <Button emphasis="quiet" bordered>Cancel</Button>
      <Button emphasis="loud" loading>Import</Button>
    </Flex>
  </Stack>
</Card>
```

In this example, only the bar and the spinner in the Import button move.

## Reduced motion slows the indicators

If a person turns on reduced motion in their operating system, the three indicators move more
slowly. They don't stop, because a busy indicator that stops looks as if the work has finished.
This is a judgment.

Nothing else in Kookie responds to the reduced motion setting, because nothing else moves on its
own.

## No component accepts an animation prop

No component accepts a duration, an easing or an animation prop. You cannot make one control
respond more slowly than another, so every screen in your app responds in the same way. To make
a control stand out, use `emphasis` or `tone`.
