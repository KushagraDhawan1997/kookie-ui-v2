Kookie shows every change of state at once. It has no transitions, and no panel animates when
it opens or closes. Only three busy indicators move, because their movement is the information
that they give you.

## Every change of state shows at once

When you hover, press or focus a control, the change shows on the next frame. Nothing fades
between the old state and the new one. The focus ring appears in its final position.

The same is true for the states that you set. A checkbox shows its tick at once. A switch
thumb goes to the other end of its track at once. The line under a tab goes to the selected tab
at once.

This is a judgment. A change that shows at once is never late, so you see the result of a press
immediately.

## A control stays still under the pointer

A control doesn't move when you hover over it or press it. Hover and press change its colours.
Its size and its position stay the same.

This applies to every control, and to a `Card` that you render as a button or a link. A
disabled control also stays where it is. Only its colours change.

## Panels open and close at once

A `Menu`, a `Select`, a `Popover` and a `Tooltip` show in their final position when they open.
A `Dialog`, an `AlertDialog` and a `Sheet` show together with the dimmed background. When you
close any of them, it goes at once.

On a narrow window, a `Shell` pane opens as a drawer beside the content and pushes the content
aside. It shows and goes at once.

A tooltip waits a moment before it opens, so it doesn't appear while your pointer only passes
over a control. This wait is a delay, not an animation.

## Some elements follow your pointer while you drag

The handle of a `Slider` stays under your pointer while you drag it. A `Sheet` follows your
finger while you drag it closed. This is not an animation, because the element moves only
while you move it.

Scrolling with a wheel, a trackpad or a finger is the browser's own, and Kookie doesn't change
it. A `Carousel` button moves its rail at once.

## Three indicators move to show that work continues

These components move without a change of state, because the movement is their message:

- `Spinner`, which also shows inside a `Button` that has `loading`
- `Progress` with `value={null}`
- `Attachment`, while it works on a file and has no progress value

Use one of these three when you can't say how much of the work is done. When you know the
fraction, give `Progress` a number. The bar then shows the fraction, and it changes only when
the number changes.

```tsx
<Card size="3">
  <Stack gap="4">
    <Heading size="6">Import files</Heading>
    <Progress value={null} aria-label="Importing files" />
    <Text size="2" emphasis="medium">Reading 24 files</Text>
    <Flex gap="3" justify="flex-end">
      <Button emphasis="quiet" bordered>Cancel</Button>
      <Button emphasis="loud" loading>Import</Button>
    </Flex>
  </Stack>
</Card>
```

In this example, only the bar and the spinner in the Import button move.

## Reduced motion slows the indicators

A person can ask their operating system for reduced motion. Then the three indicators move
more slowly. They don't stop, because a busy indicator that stops looks finished. This is a
judgment.

Nothing else responds to reduced motion, because nothing else in Kookie moves on its own.

## No component takes an animation prop

No component takes a duration, an easing or an animation prop. You can't make one control
respond more slowly than another, so every screen in your app responds in the same way.

To make a control stand out, use `emphasis` or `tone`. Don't use movement to get attention.
