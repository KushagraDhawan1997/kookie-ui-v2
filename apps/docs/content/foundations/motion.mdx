Kookie shows every change of state at once, with no transitions and no animation when a panel
opens or closes. Only three busy indicators move, because their movement is the information
they carry.

## Every change of state shows at once

When you hover, press or focus a control, the change shows on the next frame. Nothing fades
between the old state and the new one, and the focus ring appears in its final position.

The states you set behave the same way. A checkbox shows its tick at once, a switch thumb
arrives at the other end of its track at once, and the line under a tab reaches the selected tab
at once.

This is a judgment: a change that shows at once is never late, so you see the result of a press
immediately.

## A control stays still under the pointer

A control does not move when you hover over it or press it, because hover and press change its
colours while its size and position stay the same.

This applies to every control, including a `Card` that you render as a button or a link. A
disabled control also holds its place and changes only its colours.

## Panels open and close at once

A `Menu`, a `Select`, a `Popover` and a `Tooltip` appear in their final position, while a
`Dialog`, an `AlertDialog` and a `Sheet` appear together with the dimmed background. Closing any
of them removes it at once.

On a narrow window, a `Shell` pane opens as a drawer beside the content and pushes the content
aside, appearing and leaving at once as well.

A tooltip waits a moment before it opens, so that it stays hidden while your pointer merely
passes over a control. That wait is a delay rather than an animation.

## Some elements follow your pointer while you drag

The handle of a `Slider` stays under your pointer while you drag it, and a `Sheet` follows your
finger while you drag it closed. Neither counts as an animation, because the element moves only
while you move it.

Scrolling with a wheel, a trackpad or a finger belongs to the browser, and Kookie leaves it
alone. A `Carousel` button moves its rail at once.

## Three indicators move to show that work continues

These components move without a change of state, because the movement carries their message:

- `Spinner`, which also shows inside a `Button` that has `loading`
- `Progress` with `value={null}`
- `Attachment`, while it works on a file and has no progress value

Use one of these three when you cannot say how much of the work is done. Give `Progress` a
number once you know the fraction, and the bar then shows that fraction and changes only when
the number changes.

```tsx
<Card size="3">
  <Stack gap="4">
    <Heading size="6">Import files</Heading>
    <Progress value={null} aria-label="Importing files" />
    <Text size="2" emphasis="medium">Reading 24 files</Text>
    <Flex gap="3" justify="flex-end">
      <Button emphasis="quiet" bordered>Cancel</Button>
      <Button emphasis="loud" loading>Import</Button>
    </Flex>
  </Stack>
</Card>
```

In this example, only the bar and the spinner in the Import button move.

## Reduced motion slows the indicators

A person who asks their operating system for reduced motion receives the three indicators at a
slower speed. They do not stop, because a busy indicator that stops looks finished, which is a
judgment.

Nothing else responds to reduced motion, because nothing else in Kookie moves on its own.

## No component takes an animation prop

No component accepts a duration, an easing or an animation prop, so you cannot make one control
respond more slowly than another, and every screen in your app responds in the same way.

Use `emphasis` or `tone` to make a control stand out, rather than movement.
