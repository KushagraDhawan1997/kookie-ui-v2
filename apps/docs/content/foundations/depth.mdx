There is no elevation axis. No component takes a shadow, and no call site chooses one.

`depth` is a `Theme` axis with two values. It says whether this application has a light
source. Each family then decides whether it casts a shadow, and the system decides that once.

## Set depth once, at the root

```tsx
<Theme depth="elevated">
  <App />
</Theme>
```

`elevated` is the default. The world has a light source. Surfaces sit above the page, the focal
control catches light, and grips lift.

`flat` means the world has no light source. Line and fill then show every separation.

People read light as a property of the room, not of one object. Two cards on one screen, one
raised and one not, describe two rooms. So the axis sits on the `Theme`, and no component takes
a `shadow` prop, an `elevation` prop or a `raised` boolean.

## What casts a shadow

These cast: `Card` and the panes that look like it, a **loud** button, the grips, and `Kbd`.

These do not cast: `Surface`, fields, text areas, medium and quiet buttons, marks, progress
bars, separators and tab bars.

Depth separates the focal element and the floating element. Everything else sits in the plane or
on it. Three of those refusals change what you write.

- **A ground casts nothing.** A `Surface` is what objects sit on. It is not an object. Put a
  `Card` inside a `Surface` when you need the object to lift. The ground under it stays put.
- **A field is a well.** A field is content in a plane, not a plane above one. To raise a form,
  put the form in a `Card`. The fields stay recessed inside it.
- **A medium fill sits on the plane.** Rank two buttons with `emphasis`, not with depth. A loud
  button casts a shadow and catches light. A medium button does neither, so the rank reads.

A checked mark catches light and never casts a shadow. A mark is a well that fills, not a raised
key.

## A region can leave the light

Nest a `Theme` when one region of an elevated application needs no light.

```tsx
<Theme depth="elevated">
  <Stack gap="5">
    <Card size="3">
      <Stack gap="3">
        <Heading size="6">Production</Heading>
        <Text size="3" emphasis="medium">Deployed 4 minutes ago.</Text>
        <Button emphasis="loud">Promote build</Button>
      </Stack>
    </Card>

    <Theme depth="flat" render={<section />}>
      <Card size="3">
        <Text size="3" emphasis="medium">Archived environments</Text>
      </Card>
    </Theme>
  </Stack>
</Theme>
```

The first `Card` lifts and its loud button catches light. The second `Card` shows its edge with
a hairline. Nothing inside the nested `Theme` casts a shadow, including a menu that opens from
it.

## The palette is ordered by reach

The palette has five rows. Row 1 is an inset well. The other four are drops.

Each drop uses one anatomy at a different height: a contact line under the edge, which makes the
shadow read as sharp, and halos pulled in under the element by negative spread, so the depth
falls below the element instead of spreading sideways.

**A smaller element casts a smaller shadow.** A shadow that is too large makes the element read
as swollen. A card is a larger box than a menu, so the surface role uses a row with a longer
reach than the floating role. The relationship between size and reach is an observation. The row
values are a judgment.

`--shadow-1` to `--shadow-5` are public tokens. They are a resource, not an axis. Components
reach them through the world's chrome roles, never by name. For a block that the package does
not ship, use the visible escape:

```tsx
<Box style={{ boxShadow: "var(--shadow-3)" }}>Custom pane</Box>
```

`Box` takes no `shadow` prop. `Box` is layout, and a shadow is paint.

## A popup must show its edge

A popup covers the content under it, so the reader has to be able to see where it ends.

In `elevated`, the shadow shows that edge. In `flat`, nothing casts a shadow, including menus
and dialogs, so a popup draws a hairline instead. Two cues
for one fact is one cue too many.

A `Dialog` casts no shadow of its own in either world. **The scrim is the separation.** The whole
viewport goes dark, which shows the coverage more completely than a shadow does. The panel
keeps the world's ordinary surface chrome, which is the shadow a `Card` wears.

## The grips cast in both worlds

The slider grip, the switch thumb, the chosen segment of a segmented control and `Kbd` cast a
shadow in every world, including `flat`.

The shape carries the role here, as it does for the radius exceptions. A grip that does not sit
above its rail stops reading as a grip. A grip's position is the value it sets, so if it stops
reading as a grip the control stops working. Every platform ships the handle with a shadow in every context.

`Kbd` is the opposite case. A key presses into its surface, so its depth is relief and not lift.

"Always" describes the world, not the state. A disabled grip drops its shadow and dims. It keeps
its fill, because a grey grip removes the reading instead of quieting it.
