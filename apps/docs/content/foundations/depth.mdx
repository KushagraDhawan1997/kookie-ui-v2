`depth` is a Theme setting with two values, and it answers one question: does this app have
light in it? No component takes a shadow prop, and no screen picks one. Each kind of component
decides for itself whether it casts a shadow, and it decides once.

## Set depth once, at the root

```tsx
<Theme depth="elevated">
  <App />
</Theme>
```

`elevated` is the default, and it means the app has a light source. Cards sit above the page,
the main action catches light, and handles lift.

`flat` means the app has no light source. Lines and fills do all the separating instead.

This lives on the Theme because people see light as a property of the room rather than of one
object. Two cards on one screen, one raised and one not, describe two different rooms. So
there's no `shadow` prop, `elevation` prop, or `raised` boolean.

## What casts a shadow

These cast: `Card` and the panels that look like one, a **loud** button, the handles on sliders
and switches, and `Kbd`.

These don't: `Surface`, text fields, text areas, medium and quiet buttons, checkboxes,
progress bars, separators, and tab bars.

Only two things get a shadow: the thing you're meant to act on, and the thing floating above
everything else. Three of those refusals change what you write.

- **A ground casts nothing.** A `Surface` is what objects sit on, not an object itself. If you
  want something to lift, put a `Card` inside the Surface. The ground beneath it stays put.
- **A field is carved in, not raised.** To lift a form, put the form in a Card. The fields stay
  recessed inside it.
- **A medium button sits flat.** Rank two buttons with `emphasis`, not with depth. The loud
  one casts a shadow and catches light, and the medium one does neither. That difference shows
  the ranking.

A ticked checkbox catches light and never casts a shadow, because checking it fills a recess
rather than lifting anything.

**A disabled card loses its shadow.** A shadow says a thing sits above the page and can be
picked up. A disabled card can't be, so it goes back down to the page.

## Turn off the light for one region

Nest a Theme when a region of a raised app should have no light in it.

```tsx
<Theme depth="elevated">
  <Stack gap="5">
    <Card size="3">
      <Stack gap="3">
        <Heading size="6">Production</Heading>
        <Text size="3" emphasis="medium">Deployed 4 minutes ago.</Text>
        <Button emphasis="loud">Promote build</Button>
      </Stack>
    </Card>

    <Theme depth="flat" render={<section />}>
      <Card size="3">
        <Text size="3" emphasis="medium">Archived environments</Text>
      </Card>
    </Theme>
  </Stack>
</Theme>
```

The first card lifts and its loud button catches light. The second shows its edge with a thin
line instead. Nothing inside the nested Theme casts a shadow, including a menu opened from it.

## Bigger boxes get longer shadows

Five shadow values exist. The first is a recess, and the other four are drops.

Each drop is built the same way at a different height. A tight contact line under the edge
keeps it looking sharp rather than smudged. Softer layers are pulled inward, so the depth
falls *under* the element instead of spreading out sideways.

**A smaller thing casts a smaller shadow**, or it looks swollen. A card is a bigger box than
a menu, so cards use a longer-reaching value than floating panels do. That relationship is an
observation. The exact values are a judgment.

`--shadow-1` to `--shadow-5` are public, but they're a resource rather than a setting.
Components never name them directly. They go through the theme. If you're building something
the package doesn't ship, use `style`:

```tsx
<Box style={{ boxShadow: "var(--shadow-3)" }}>Custom pane</Box>
```

`Box` has no `shadow` prop, because Box is layout and a shadow is paint.

## A popup has to show where it ends

A popup covers what is underneath it, so you have to be able to see its edge.

In a raised app, the shadow does that. In a flat app nothing casts a shadow, menus and
dialogs included, so a popup draws a thin line instead.

A `Dialog` casts no shadow of its own in either case, because **the dimmed background is the
separation**. The whole window going dark shows what the dialog covers more completely than a
shadow could. The panel keeps the ordinary card shadow of the current depth setting.

## Handles cast a shadow even in a flat app

The slider handle, the switch thumb, the selected segment of a segmented control, and `Kbd`
cast a shadow everywhere, flat apps included.

This is the same idea as the four shapes that survive `radius="none"`: the appearance
carries the meaning. A handle that doesn't sit above its track stops looking like a handle,
and a handle's position *is* the value it's setting, so the control stops working. Every
platform ships handles with a shadow in every context.

`Kbd` is the opposite case. A key presses *into* its surface, so its depth is a recess rather
than a lift.

A disabled handle still drops its shadow and dims, but it keeps its colour. A grey handle in
a grey track would be hard to see.
