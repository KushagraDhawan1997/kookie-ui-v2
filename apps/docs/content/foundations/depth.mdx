There is no elevation axis. No component takes a shadow, no call site chooses one, and `depth`
is a Theme identity with two values — an app-level statement about whether this world has light
in it. What casts and what does not is then a fact about each family, decided once.

## Elevation is deleted

The spec once carried a four-level shadow ladder: flat, raised, floating, overlay. It died on
first contact with the eye, and the post-mortem found something worse than an ugly ladder —
**nothing had ever chosen elevation at a call site.** A Card is never floating, a Menu is never
flat, a Dialog is always an overlay. A prop nobody varies is a component fact wearing an axis's
clothes.

One word replaces it:

```tsx
<Theme depth="elevated">
  <App />
</Theme>
```

`elevated` is the default, and it says the world has a light source: surfaces sit up, the focal
control catches light, grips lift. `flat` says it does not, and separation goes back to line and
fill.

`--shadow-1` through `--shadow-5` are public tokens, but they are a **resource, not an axis**.
Components reach them only through the world's chrome roles, and a law walks every shipped
stylesheet asserting that no rule names a shadow row directly. For a block you own, the escape
is deliberately visible in review rather than blessed as a prop:

```tsx
<Box style={{ boxShadow: "var(--shadow-3)" }}>Custom pane</Box>
```

Box carried a typed `shadow` prop for one day and it died as a taxonomy leak: Box is layout,
shadow is paint.

## The palette is ordered by reach, not by rank

Five rows on the system's one index shape. Row 1 is the inset well; the rest are drops, each the
same anatomy at a different height — a contact line under the edge, which is what makes a shadow
read sharp, plus halos pulled in under the element by negative spread, so depth falls below
rather than haloing sideways.

The ordering is by **reach**, and that is the part worth carrying away: a smaller caster owes a
smaller shadow, or it reads swollen. A card is a bigger box than a menu, so the surface role
sits on a longer-reach row than the floating one. That reverses an earlier call — that a
floating pane must out-cast everything under it — and the reversal is the honest reading: a
panel's authority comes from covering what is beneath it, not from out-shadowing a card three
times its size. Size against reach is an observation; the rows themselves are taste, judged by
eye, and named as taste.

## What casts, and what does not

Casting: `Card` and the panes that wear its identity, the **loud** rung of a button, the grips
and the key cap. That is the whole list, and each entry had to be argued into it.

Not casting: `Surface`, fields and text areas, the medium and quiet rungs, marks, progress bars,
separators and tab bars.

Depth separates the **focal** and the **floating**; everything else sits in or on the plane.
Three refusals are worth stating outright, because each looks arbitrary until you have the
sentence.

- **A ground throws nothing.** A `Surface` is what objects sit on, not an object — a hole in a
  plane casts no shadow. A Card inside it lifts; the bed it lifts off does not.
- **A field is a well.** It is content of a plane, not a plane above one — carved in rather than
  raised off. A recessed thing cannot cast a drop shadow, and no platform shades an input. This
  question was reopened four times and closed in the same place every time.
- **A medium fill is a wash on the plane.** Measured beside a loud button in the playground, the
  two floated identically and rank stopped reading. Loud casts and catches light; medium does
  neither.

A checked mark catches light and never casts, for the same reason: a mark is a well that fills,
not a raised key.

## A floating layer states its coverage

A popup covers what is under it, and it owes the reader a boundary that says so.

In `elevated`, the floating chrome says it. In `flat`, nothing casts — menus and dialogs
included — and a pane states its coverage in flat's own vocabulary, the hairline. Flat kept a
half-faded cast under popups for a while because it had no other way to bound a covering pane;
the day the hairline came back, that cast became a second voice saying one thing and was
retired.

A Dialog casts nothing of its own in either world, and that is not an oversight: **the scrim is
the separation.** The whole viewport going dark states the coverage more completely than any
shadow could, so what survives on the panel is the world's ordinary surface chrome — the same
shadow a Card wears.

## The grips' exception

The slider grip, the switch thumb, the chosen segment of a segmented control and `Kbd` all cast
in **both** worlds, `flat` included.

This is role semantics, the same shape as the radius exceptions and held to the same standard. A
grip that does not sit above its rail stops reading as a grip, and a grip's position is the
value — losing the reading loses the control. Every platform ships the handle shadowed in every
context. `Kbd` is the mirror case: a key is pressed *into* its surface, so its depth is relief
rather than lift.

"Always" is about the world, never about state. A disabled grip stands its cast down and dims
instead of vanishing, because greying a grip erases the reading rather than standing it down.

## What you cannot do

There is no shadow prop, no elevation prop and no `raised` boolean on any component. If a region
inside an elevated app genuinely wants no light, that is a nested Theme:

```tsx
<Theme depth="flat" render={<section />}>
  <Card>Archived</Card>
</Theme>
```

The refusal has a shape worth naming. This system will give you an app-level identity and a
visible escape; it will not give you a per-element knob for something a reader perceives as a
property of the world rather than of the thing in front of them.
